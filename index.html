The kitchen

A menu that follows the market.

Twelve plates from breakfast through late evening — bowls, mains, sweets, and drinks built around what is ripe, not what is fashionable.

Signatures

Three plates we cook every day.

Bowls

The Bloom Bowl

Farro, roasted beets, citrus, and a herb yogurt that tastes like a garden after rain. It is the plate most guests return for — bright, filling, and never heavy.

Main course

Lemon-Thyme Chicken

Slow-roasted until the skin is lacquered and the meat stays juicy. Served with wilted greens, pan juices, and a squeeze of preserved lemon.

Desserts

Honey & Berry Tart

A thin pastry shell, whipped mascarpone, and berries warmed with wildflower honey. Sweet enough for celebration, quiet enough for a Tuesday.

The house

Food arranged with the same care as a bouquet.

Daisybloom began as a small kitchen that treated produce the way florists treat stems: season first, then composition, then restraint. We still cook that way.

Every plate starts with what the market looks like that morning. We keep the ingredient list short, the technique honest, and the dining room unhurried. Premium, but never precious — a table you can visit twice a week.

Fresh ingredients

Herbs, citrus, dairy, and greens sourced from nearby growers. If it is not ripe, it is not on the menu.

Thoughtful prep

Stocks simmered overnight, bread baked at dawn, dressings whisked to order. Time is an ingredient.

Simple plates

Few components, clearly cooked. The goal is flavor you can name, not garnish you cannot.

Modern table

Breakfast through late supper, eat-in or takeaway, made for a city that still wants a proper meal.
